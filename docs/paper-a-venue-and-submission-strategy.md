## Recommendation and readiness

**Primary: TMLR, after scientific completion and an A/B overlap audit. Contingency: a future officially announced NeurIPS Evaluations & Datasets (E&D) cycle, only if Paper A remains unpublished and is no longer under archival review elsewhere. Current submission readiness: NO-GO for both.**

This is a project judgment, not a venue acceptance prediction. TMLR includes task formalization, performance-assessment methods and reproducibility studies in its scope. Its criteria prioritize supported claims and clear lessons for an interested ML audience; a new architecture or state-of-the-art score is unnecessary. Rolling review accommodates the unresolved empirical timeline. The space available also suits the distinction between reconstructed evidence, executable contracts and unavailable artifacts. [TMLR editorial policies](https://jmlr.org/tmlr/editorial-policies.html), [acceptance criteria](https://jmlr.org/tmlr/acceptance-criteria.html), [FAQ](https://jmlr.org/tmlr/faq.html).

E&D is an especially direct topical fit for C1–C8: evaluation assumptions, benchmark audits, documentation, protocols and negative findings. It is the contingency because its next usable call and deadline are unconfirmed, its concise format requires a mature synthesis, and its artifact requirements need careful reconciliation with the project's rights/capability boundary. Neither venue justifies stronger claims. [2026 E&D call](https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2026/CallForEvaluationsDatasets).

The accepted drafts complete methodological groundwork, not the complete paper mission. Independent qualification of the prospective real population, the authorized frozen demonstration, publication positioning and a reviewable supplement remain outstanding. An honestly scoped methodology-only submission might eventually be defensible, but omitting the prospective demonstration would change the Paper A mission and needs an explicit scope decision in issue 346. This record does not make that change.

## Double-blind review and the public repository

TMLR uses double-blind, public reviewing; papers become public during its review process. The manuscript and supplements must be anonymized. TMLR permits identified preprints at any time but prohibits linking the anonymous submission to an identified version. Submissions are CC BY 4.0 from submission onward, with authors retaining copyright. [Editorial policies](https://jmlr.org/tmlr/editorial-policies.html), [author guide](https://jmlr.org/tmlr/author-guide.html).

E&D's 2026 default is double-blind. Dataset-centered exceptions for scientific/ethical impediments can request single-blind review; the PDF still uses the anonymous format. Existing-codebase benchmarks must make a best effort to anonymize. A public GitHub project alone should not be treated as automatic permission to submit identified code. [E&D call](https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2026/CallForEvaluationsDatasets), [FAQ](https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2026/EvaluationsDatasetsFAQ).

Practical preparation for either venue, in subsequent submission work:

1. Preserve the canonical public repository and its provenance. Prepare a pinned anonymous review snapshot or anonymous hosting interface without changing scientific IDs, results or locks.
2. Inspect the review copy for owner URLs, README badges, author metadata, acknowledgments, issue links, local paths, Git history and identifying archive/PDF metadata. This strategy document's Linear links and canonical development branch are administrative context, not anonymous review material.
3. Preserve required license/attribution notices. If anonymization conflicts with those obligations, resolve the presentation with the venue; never strip notices or promise perfect anonymity for a public project.
4. Test anonymous access and the documented public reproduction path. Mark unavailable historical checkpoints/data explicitly. Supply de-anonymized release/citation links at the appropriate final stage.

These are review-package tasks, not instructions to rewrite repository history or conduct security engineering now. The existing public repo and any preprint may make authors inferable; best-effort compliance remains necessary.

**TMLR is not an extension venue for an accepted NeurIPS Paper A.** Its policy prohibits reuse of text, figures or results from the authors' published, accepted or parallel archival papers, and explicitly rejects expanded conference versions. Preprints and venues explicitly declared non-archival in writing are exceptions. [Editorial policies](https://jmlr.org/tmlr/editorial-policies.html).

NeurIPS treats overlapping-author archival submissions as prior work: if one makes the other too incremental, both may be rejected. Submitting the same work elsewhere while it remains under NeurIPS review is explicitly prohibited. [Handbook](https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2026/MainTrackHandbook).

Operational rule: only one active archival route for Paper A. Switch to the contingency only after a recorded rejection or confirmed withdrawal ending the first review; re-audit policies and prior versions before resubmission. Acceptance ends the same-work contingency. Non-archival workshops need a written non-archival declaration, not an assumption based on the event name. Maintain version, review-status and asset ownership records in [revision tracking / issue 357](https://linear.app/alignerr-cmj/issue/34b994f2-c1d1-4331-a4e7-4f21aa3e0951).

## Paper A, Paper B and software: ownership and non-overlap

The [Paper B mission / issue 312](https://linear.app/alignerr-cmj/issue/6b711216-2b3b-4f7b-9485-10ebad96784c) was also read directly. It owns empirical football-motion interpretation. Sharing a repository, source cohort or frozen protocol does not by itself establish distinct paper contributions. Rewording the same result or moving it to a supplement does not remove overlap.

| Output | Intended original contribution | Boundary to enforce before archival submission |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paper A | Benchmark/task semantics; evaluation contracts; historical reconstruction and bounded negative outcomes; capability/provenance methodology; a prospective workflow demonstration. | Own the methodology exposition, genealogy/contract figures and any demonstration results actually published here. Do not publish Paper B's primary information-regime contrasts, representation-effect narrative, empirical generalization/robustness findings or full campaign analysis here. |
| Paper B | Qualified prospective empirical findings: future-context, representation, split, simple/learned model and measurement/robustness questions, with confirmatory/exploratory status fixed by the study. | Cite Paper A's definitions rather than repeat its original exposition/figures. Publish genuinely additional results and interpretations; do not relabel A's demonstration as a new experiment. Planned questions not supported by the frozen study need explicit follow-up versions, not silently added contrasts. |
| Future JOSS/software paper | Software purpose, implementation/design choices, installation, examples, sustainable reuse and demonstrated research use. | Cite the scientific papers; do not duplicate their novel methodology exposition, figures, numerical results or scientific conclusions. JOSS permits related science/methods publications but requires disclosure and substantial implementation effort. [JOSS submission requirements](https://joss.readthedocs.io/en/latest/submitting.html). |

**Unresolved overlap blocker:** the current plan intentionally feeds the same frozen campaign into A's demonstration and B's primary matrix. No approved result/asset partition exists yet. Recommend reserving the four primary learned information contrasts and the full comparative empirical analysis for B, while keeping A's demonstration to the minimum needed to establish the workflow. This is an ownership recommendation, not evidence that the remaining numerical results are automatically disjoint. Repeated deterministic controls across regimes, absolute/displacement views of the same predictions, different aggregation views and supplements must all be counted in the overlap audit.

Before either archival submission, issue 346 and [Paper B claim locking / issue 347](https://linear.app/alignerr-cmj/issue/570d37bc-e77f-4e76-a513-9bbccf456650) must map every result, figure, table and substantive claim to one paper. Identify shared input artifacts separately from published results. If A's minimum demonstration and B's required comparisons still materially reuse results, resolve this by consolidating the overlapping scientific material into one archival paper or explicitly narrowing a paper's mission. Do not manufacture extra experiments, change endpoints, or assume cross-citation overrides TMLR's prohibition. Ambiguous boundaries require editorial clarification before submission; no editor contact is undertaken here.

Sequence A's review first and stabilize its actual archival content before finalizing B's archival package. Any later B submission must stand as an independent scientific contribution, with the existing paper/preprint disclosed and cited appropriately. If concurrent review is later proposed, require a fresh content audit and compliance with both venues; this strategy recommends sequential review. Cite common software/protocol releases rather than duplicate original prose or result panels. Keep a canonical asset record of input/result hashes, intended paper and archival status, and update it after reviewer-requested changes.

Defer JOSS until the software maturity gate is independently met. Current JOSS requirements include sustained public development for more than six months, actual research use, open-source practices and a feature-complete package; local commit age alone does not prove public history. Its paper must live with the software, and related publications must be disclosed. These requirements have not been audited for this project here. [JOSS submission requirements](https://joss.readthedocs.io/en/latest/submitting.html). Deferring its openly identified review also avoids adding a new author-identification route during Paper A review.
