# Reconstructed benchmark transitions

This graph separates benchmark identity, data state, evaluator state, release state, and research lineage. A task appearing later in the project does not by itself establish a predecessor relationship.

## Workload and synthetic sensor research

`workload_performance_state/whole_session_performance_state` is a partial historical study. It is non-executable and has no direct model bindings. `workload_performance_state/signed_tangential_acceleration_estimation` is a separate, invalidated synthetic formulation with a 10 Hz target and seven diagnostic model bindings. The evidence does not make those models whole-session experts or establish a benchmark-version edge between the objects.

The evidence supports a research-task lineage from `workload_performance_state/signed_tangential_acceleration_estimation` to `multimodal_state_estimation/multimodal_sensor_state_reconstruction`. This is a successor synthetic research task, not a benchmark release or version transition. Exact simulator identity remains unknown. The sensor-state pilot's negative result applies to its recovered targets and selection gate; it does not establish that the wider research family is impossible.

The move from `multimodal_state_estimation/multimodal_sensor_state_reconstruction` to `conditional_multi_agent_motion_prediction` is a project-level source-regime and scientific-question switch. No model or data successor edge is established.

## Absolute position and repaired measurement state

`conditional_multi_agent_motion_prediction/absolute_position_prediction` retains one benchmark identity when its measurement state is repaired. The repair changes source eligibility, sampled measurements, and resulting dataset membership; it keeps the scientific contract: five seconds of history, a three-second horizon, a five-hertz grid, absolute XY for 11 target players, realized future opponent and ball XY as supplied context, and future target-team XY withheld.

Metric, scorer, and calibration versions belong to evaluator identity. Updating those states alone does not create a benchmark identity. Displacement calibration parity remains unknown; no calibration values are inferred here.

## Absolute position and displacement

`conditional_multi_agent_motion_prediction/origin_relative_displacement_prediction` is a separate benchmark from `conditional_multi_agent_motion_prediction/absolute_position_prediction`. Both use the same scientific task family, scene history, realized future opponent and ball context, information boundary, five-second/three-second temporal contract, five-hertz grid, target entities, repaired measurement state, public population and split roles, and raw metric family.

The research question, target representation, and reference frame change: absolute pitch XY becomes each player's future displacement from their causal forecast-origin position. Those scientific changes require a new benchmark identity. Exact scorer-wrapper parity, calibration parity, the full baseline ladder, exact architecture parity, and model-selection parity remain unknown.

## Future Response Forecasting

`future_response_forecasting` remains a research family with no recovered benchmark identity or historical research object. It has no supported predecessor among the workload, acceleration, sensor-state, or conditional-motion work. Conditional motion supplies realized future opponent and ball paths; Future Response would require ex-ante available future exposure. They describe different information boundaries. No finding here claims Future Response research is scientifically impossible.
